Beauty Is in the Eye of the Beholder

Can a machine learning model tell whether two people are meant to be together? Upload a group photo, or several photos, and see which people it pairs up.

The app finds every face, rates every possible pair on each factor, and shows the likely couples, the ratings behind them, and sliders to change what counts.

      How the match works

      What it reads

      • Estimated age and apparent gender
      • An attractiveness rating, when its model is installed
      • Outfit colour and, if you switch it on, where people stand

      How it decides

      Every possible man–woman pair is rated on each factor, and the ratings are combined into a score out of 100 using weights you can change. The best pairs are picked without using anyone twice. It’s an experiment, not proof: friends and siblings can look alike too.

      Safeguards and privacy

      • Skin colour is never used
      • Anyone who may be under 18 is never matched, and nor is anyone whose face is too small to check
      • With several photos, faces are compared with each other so the same person is counted once. They are only compared within your upload: no names, no database, nothing kept.
      • Your photo isn’t saved. Only the measurements are logged on this computer, to help fix mistakes.

      The Dating Game is an academic machine learning project. Face detection (YuNet) and same-person matching (SFace) come from the OpenCV Model Zoo; age and expression models come from the ONNX Model Zoo.
